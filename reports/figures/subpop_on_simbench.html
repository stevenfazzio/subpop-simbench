SubPOP adapters on SimBench

Four language models, each scored on SimBench with and without its SubPOP adapter, on identical prompts. Every dumbbell runs from the base model's score to the score with the adapter. A score of 100 matches the human answer distribution; 0 is no better than answering uniformly; below 0 is worse than uniform.

For scale: a predictor that never reads the question, and knows only each dataset's typical answer shape, scores 19.4. Squares and vertical ticks mark that baseline.

Retraining the Mistral-7B adapter

SubPOP's released adapters fit only their own four base models. As a check that the recipe can be rerun, the Mistral-7B adapter was trained again here from SubPOP's data, for 20 epochs. Two checkpoints were scored: epoch 7, which had the lowest loss on held-out questions, and the final one. Both land within 2.2 points of the released adapter under every prompt format, and on 85% to 99% of items their predictions are nearer the released adapter's than the base model's. The format selector does not apply here: the panels are the three formats.

The held-out loss stops falling after a few epochs while the training loss keeps going, yet the final epoch scores 1.5 points higher than epoch 7 on SubPOP's exact format. The released adapter's 0.034 on these held-out questions is not a target to compare with: it is below the 0.057 that comes from knowing each question's average answer, so it has probably trained on most of them.

  • Scores are means over items of 100 × (1 − TVD / norm), where TVD is the total variation distance between the model's and the humans' answer distributions and norm is the dataset's mean TVD between humans and uniform.
  • Model answers are read from next-token probabilities over the option letters, in bfloat16, one forward pass per prompt.
  • The question-blind predictors use human answers to other questions: the average answer shape of every other question with the same number of options, either in the item's own dataset (19.4) or across the whole benchmark (8.4). No model gets these for free; they show how much score answer shape alone is worth.
  • Published scores are from the SimBench paper (arXiv:2510.17516) and apply to SimBench's own prompt format only. Its instruction-tuned models were asked to state a distribution in words, and its open-weight base models were run 8-bit or 4-bit quantised, so the comparison is indicative.
  • SimBench's human answers for its 500 MoralMachine items look reversed (open issue on SimBench's repository). In the 51 items that pit only animals against only people, the majority is recorded as choosing to kill the people in 50. This page scores the data as published. With those answers reversed, no overall score here moves by more than 2.1 points and no adapter scores above 2 on MoralMachine. If the published scores were computed on the same data, they may move by more.
  • On SubPOP's own prompt format, which exists for 964 OpinionQA items, the adapters score Mistral-7B 76.6 (base 28.4); Llama-2-7B 75.9 (base 27.1); Llama-2-13B 76.0 (base 25.2); Llama-3-70B 79.3 (base 53.5).
